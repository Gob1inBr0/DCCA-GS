# Revised Abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) achieves high-quality real-time novel-view rendering with explicit Gaussian primitives, but the per-primitive storage of geometry, covariance, opacity, and appearance grows rapidly with scene scale, making storage and transmission a deployment bottleneck for large-scale UAV and multi-view capture. This contribution describes DCCA-GS, built on the HAC++ anchor-hash entropy-coding framework. DCCA-GS keeps the HAC++ main pipeline (structured anchors, hash-grid context, conditional entropy model, arithmetic coding, G-PCC geometry) and adds four evaluated components: (1) decoder-reproducible content-adaptive formula quantization, in which every quantization step is recomputed on both ends from decoder-reconstructable structural quantities, so no per-anchor step data is transmitted; (2) render-sensitivity supervision, a training-only mechanism that steers the complexity network without changing the bitstream; (3) mixed-precision post-training quantization of the decoder MLPs, with a per-group bit-width assignment derived from measured sensitivity to rendering quality and entropy modeling, charged to the reported size; and (4) training-side anchor sparsity with ADMM projection, which imposes a hard anchor budget during training and re-adapts the surviving anchors under that budget. Components (1) and (2) are jointly designed, and their interaction is explicitly analyzed in the ablation study. On the 4-28 UAV scene, the 110k-iteration operating point with MLP quantization reaches 28.82 dB PSNR at 5.49 MiB decode-required size, versus 28.31 dB at 6.95 MiB for the paper-reported HAC++ point on the same scene; on Deep Blending, playroom reaches 30.73 dB at 4.22 MiB and drjohnson 30.04 dB at 6.85 MiB. We also report quantitative analyses of codec efficiency (actual/estimated bits close to 1.0), a KL-redundancy lower bound for the scaling field, bitstream composition, rate-distortion behavior over lambda and training steps, and documented negative results (context conditioning below 2.3%, residual coding, and sensitivity side information). All bitstreams pass bit-exact standalone decoding verification.

# Revised Introduction (contributions part)

Our contributions are as follows.

1. **Decoder-reproducible content-adaptive formula quantization.** Quantization steps are generated on the fly from decoder-reconstructable structural quantities—local anchor density, predicted scaling anisotropy, predicted offset energy, and active mask ratio. The image-side statistics used during development are excluded from the formula mode and are therefore neither transmitted nor required at decode time; the bitstream carries no per-anchor step array. Training uses a straight-through estimator so that the quantization path is shared by training, encoding, and decoding.

2. **Render-sensitivity supervision.** The complexity network is trained to match a bounded, exponentially smoothed target derived from render-loss gradient norms. This affects training only and adds zero side information. Although its high-rate gain is modest (about 0.1 dB), it becomes critical in the low-rate SPA regime, where removing it reduces PSNR by 0.42 dB (see Section 3.4).

3. **Mixed-precision MLP weight quantization.** We apply per-output-channel symmetric post-training quantization with static arithmetic coding. The bit-width assignment is not uniform: the complexity and deform networks are quantized to 8 bits, while the opacity, covariance, color, and grid networks remain at 16 bits. This assignment is derived from per-MLP sensitivity ablation: 8-bit opacity directly degrades rendering, and 8-bit grid degrades the entropy model and increases attribute rate, whereas complexity/deform tolerate 8 bits with negligible loss.

4. **Training-side anchor sparsity (SPA).** Unlike encode-side top-k pruning, SPA imposes a hard anchor budget during training via ADMM projection and a linear budget ramp anchored to the historical maximum anchor count. This allows surviving anchors to re-adapt under the budget; at equal anchor count it outperforms encode-side top-k pruning by 5.5 dB, while the resulting operating point trades 0.84 dB PSNR for a 74% size reduction relative to the non-SPA model (see Section 3.4).

In the implementation and experiments, components (1) and (2) are referred to as I2 and I6, respectively; they are jointly designed, and their interaction is analyzed in the ablation study. All experiments follow a strict bitstream contract with standalone decoding verification and quantitative analyses of where the bits go and how much redundancy remains; this constitutes the evaluation protocol of this work rather than a separate technical contribution.
