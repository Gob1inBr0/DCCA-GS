\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Preliminaries and the zero-side-information contract}
\label{sec:prelim}

Our production codec follows the anchor--hash architecture of HAC++~\cite{chen2025}: a scene is encoded as $N$ anchors; anchor $i$ carries a feature vector $\feat_i\in\mathbb{R}^{F}$ ($F{=}32$ in all experiments), scaling parameters, $K{=}10$ offset vectors, and a binary offset mask $\smask_i$; anchor coordinates are coded with G-PCC, and hash-grid features predicted from the decoded coordinates drive hypernetworks that output entropy parameters and per-element quantization steps.
At full precision this single-rate stream is the reference that our progressive stream must reproduce bit-exactly.

The compression objective is standard
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta}\; D(\theta) + \lambda R(\theta),
\label{eq:rd}
\end{equation}
with distortion $D$ and code length $R$.
What we change is the \emph{shape of the output}: instead of one rate point, the encoder must emit a byte string $B$ such that every prefix of $B$ that ends at a block boundary decodes into a renderable scene, with quality monotone in the prefix length.

We call the stream \emph{zero-side-information} if decoding a prefix requires (i) no data outside the received prefix and (ii) no learned component: every quantization step, conditioning group, and probability conditioning must be a function of already-decoded data, and the only priors allowed in the stream are small static tables carried inline after content has started flowing.
This is the contract that five peers' headers do not meet (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}), and every design below serves it.

\subsection{Nested quality ladders and their residuals}
\label{sec:ladder}

For each attribute field (feature, scaling, offset), symbols are quantized at a ladder of steps $s_0 > s_1 > \dots > s_L = 1$, where $s_\ell = 2^{L-\ell}\,s_L$ within a field's ladder.
The base layer transmits $q_0 = \mathrm{round}(x / s_0)$ for every symbol; refinement layer $\ell$ transmits the residual
\begin{equation}
r_\ell \;=\; \mathrm{round}(x / s_\ell) \;-\; \tfrac{s_{\ell-1}}{s_\ell}\, q_{\ell-1},
\qquad q_\ell = q_{\ell-1}\cdot\tfrac{s_{\ell-1}}{s_\ell} + r_\ell ,
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
so the decoder accumulates $q_\ell$ layer by layer and recovers the full-precision symbol exactly when the last layer arrives.
Ladders are \emph{field-aware}: features and offsets start at $8\times$ coarsening (four tiers), while scaling---stored in the log domain, where coarse steps destroy geometry---starts at $2\times$.
In an earlier-protocol measurement, a uniform $8\times$ ladder collapsed the entry tier from 25.24 to 21.89\,dB at matched bytes; the field-aware ladder removes the collapse entirely.\footnote{Design-validation numbers in Secs.~\ref{sec:ladder}--\ref{sec:align} come from a 16-view pilot protocol in a superseded environment; they are design rationale, re-measurement under the final protocol is scheduled (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).}
Refinement residuals depend only on \emph{the same field's} coarser layers, which is what allows arbitrary fields' blocks to be truncated independently.

Layering trades selection: we also measured the alternative of transmitting only the top-$k\%$ anchors by contribution, ranked by rendering contribution.
Ranking itself works---at equal bytes it beats a random order by 5--6\,dB---but its ceiling is structural: the top 50\% of anchors still render 2.34\,dB below all anchors, because missing anchors leave holes that transmitted ones cannot fill.
Coarsening \emph{all} anchors by $4\times$ costs only 0.60\,dB.
Spatial completeness tolerates coarse attributes far better than absence, so we layer by precision and keep ranking only as an in-layer ordering device.

\subsection{Binary-decision coding with inline static tables}
\label{sec:coding}

Each refinement residual is coded as a sequence of binary decisions: nonzero flag, sign, then magnitude bits.
Binary decisions keep every probability away from 0 and 1, so a fixed-precision range coder has no zero-probability failure mode, and near-zero residuals---the overwhelming majority---cost fractions of a bit.
In the pilot protocol this coder cut the full-precision stream from 37.9 to 29.95\,MB on the same symbols, a 21\% saving over coding each residual as one alphabet symbol.

Decision probabilities come from grouped frequency tables: anchors are sorted into 32 groups by rendering contribution, and each decision is additionally conditioned on the magnitude bucket of the \emph{already-decoded} coarse symbol ($0$ / $1$ / $\ge 2$)---coarse zeros almost surely stay zero; large coarse magnitudes widen the residual.
Group ranks and bucket indices are recomputed by the decoder from received data; the tables themselves are three numbers per group (median, zero ratio, carry ratio), a few kilobytes per block, transmitted inline.
This conditioning saves 1.9--2.3\,MB per scene over unconditioned tables in the pilot protocol.
Quantization steps follow the HAC++ adaptive-quantization form $Q = Q_0\,(1{+}\tanh a)\,(1{+}\alpha\tanh z)$, where $z$ is a small network over local anchor density, predicted-scaling anisotropy, predicted offset energy, and mask activity---all recomputable at the decoder; measured agreement between encoder-used and decoder-recomputed steps is 100\% on feature/offset and 99.999\% on scaling symbols (residual differences are numeric boundary clamps).

\subsection{File layout, truncatability, and bit-exactness}
\label{sec:layout}

The file is a header (block order and per-field ladders, plain JSON) followed by blocks of $[\,\text{len}\,][\,\text{tables}\,][\,\text{payload}\,]$, ordered by layer: all fields' base layers first, then layer-1 refinements, and so on (Fig.~\ref{fig:bitstream}).
Because refinements reference only coarser layers of their own field, any block boundary is a decodable stream; we verify this by per-block assertions during decode.
The full-precision tier is checked bit-exact against the production single-rate decode of the same model, and every experiment below evaluates decoded bitstreams at each prefix, not training-time estimates.

\subsection{Ladder-aligned quantization training}
\label{sec:align}

A model trained only with Eq.~\eqref{eq:rd} spreads symbols uniformly between coarse lattice points, so the base layer---the only tier an impatient receiver sees---absorbs the full coarsening error.
Late in training we therefore add
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}} \;=\; \frac{w(t)}{|\Omega|} \sum_{(i,f)\in\Omega} \operatorname{dist}\!\left(x_{i,f},\, s_{0,f}\,\mathbb{Z}\right)^2,
\label{eq:align}
\end{equation}
the squared distance between each pre-quantization attribute $x_{i,f}$ and the base-layer lattice, ramped with $w(t)$ over a late training window (from iteration 24{,}000 of 30{,}000 in all measured runs) with gradients through quantization and masks cut off.
Aligned symbols are represented \emph{exactly} by the base layer's grid, so the entry tier gains fidelity and upper-layer residuals shrink toward zero---the same bytes buy more quality everywhere, but the benefit concentrates where the ladder is coarsest.
The penalty competes with the rendering loss in this window, which is where its full-precision cost arises; the measured trade across $\lambda$ and $w$ is in Table~\ref{tab:align}.

\subsection{Anchor-budget axis}
\label{sec:budget}

The entry size of the stream is dominated by base-layer attributes, which scale linearly with anchor count (86\% of base-layer bytes in our byte accounting; anchor geometry 8\%, fixed load 6\%).
We therefore expose a training-time budget: a GaussianSpa-style ADMM alternation~\cite{zhang2025gaussianspa} keeps a binary auxiliary state $z$ and dual variable $u$ over per-anchor scores $a_i = \mathrm{mean}(\smask_i)$,
\begin{equation}
z = \mathrm{TopK}(a + u, \kappa), \qquad
u = \mathrm{clamp}(u + a - z, \pm 1),
\label{eq:admm}
\end{equation}
prunes anchors with $z_i{=}0$, and lets survivors re-train under the budget $\kappa = r\,N_{\mathrm{ref}}$.
The budget ratio $r$ is a continuous knob on \emph{where the progressive stream starts}: smaller $r$ shrinks the entry tier and the whole file roughly linearly, while survivors re-adapt to ``this is all there is'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:budget-exp}).
$z$ and $u$ are training-only state and never enter the stream.
